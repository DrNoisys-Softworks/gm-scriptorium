# Orpiment

The dragon is the yellow of the old painters' pigment she is named for, fading to rose at the throat, and she is large enough that when she lay along the roof of the Wool Exchange her tail hung down into Tanner's Row. She speaks in complete sentences with subordinate clauses. She calls everyone in the vale "tenant," including Queen Hesper.

## What she is like

Orpiment is courteous the way a contract is courteous: every word means exactly what it says, and nothing more has been promised. She does not threaten. When the Chancellor offered her a chest of crowns in the square she thanked him, declined, and observed that she had not been asked for gold and had not asked for any. She has been patient for three hundred years and plans to be patient for another forty days.

She is curious about small made things. She asked Odda Quarrel what a bell was for, listened to the whole answer, and then asked whether it could play a tune.

## What she wants

The rent, exactly as the lease states it, delivered at the Lammas moon of Lease Year 300. She will not say what it is. "A tenant who must be told the rent," she said, "has not read the lease." If the rent is paid, she says, the lease provides for what happens next. If it is not, the lease has a clause for that too, and she has declined to say what it is in front of children.

## Dealings with the Wardens

- **Session 1.** Landed on the Wool Exchange at the Midsummer Fair, read the term and the due date aloud, and gave the Crown until the Lammas moon. Named Kit Farrow "tenant" and gave him a scale as a receipt.
- **Session 2.** Not seen. The Tally Cliffs rang with something like humming for most of the afternoon.
- **Session 3.** Seen from the Sallow Hills, circling the cliffs at dusk, twice.

## GM Notes

Run Orpiment as a set piece, never as a fight. Do not roll initiative for her. If a tenant attacks her, climb a ladder rather than ending the campaign: the first time, she moves them somewhere else, gently, and says aloud that the Tenant's people are troubling her rest (she has already said it once, to Sir Ivo, in public). The second time, she raises the Tenant's conduct with Queen Hesper. Only after that do consequences under the lease follow.

The rent is a song never sung before, made by the vale and given freely, to be sung to her at the Lammas moon. Orpiment cannot sing: her voice breaks glass at a hum and slates at a note. Maudry sang to her on the cliffs the day they signed, and it was the first thing in a thousand years that was made for her rather than taken from her. That is why she wept, and why the rent is what it is.

She did not fly away after the signing. She went to sleep in the cave under the ledge and woke once a year, at midsummer night, to cut the year's groove; the vale took the new groove for the Founder's luck. She has not cut the three-hundredth. She will cut it on the signing stone when the rent is paid, and not before.

She will honour every clause to the letter, including the assignment clause on the counterpart's sealed second leaf: whoever tenders the rent holds the vale for a further term. She would not like it to be anyone but the Crown. She will not bend the terms to prevent it, and she will not warn anyone, because a lessor who explains the lease to one tenant has favoured them over another.
