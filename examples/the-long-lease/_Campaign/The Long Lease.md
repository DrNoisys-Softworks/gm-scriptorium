# The Long Lease

Three hundred years ago, a shepherd queen called [[Queen Maudry]] climbed the cliffs at the head of a green valley and asked the dragon who lived there whether the valley could be rented. The dragon, [[Orpiment]], said yes. The lease ran for three hundred summers, with the rent payable at the end.

Everyone forgot. The songs said the Founder drove the dragon away, and the statue in the square shows her pointing her sword at the sky.

At midsummer of Lease Year 300, the dragon came back for the rent.

## The premise

The players are the **Wardens of the Lease**: four people who happened to be closest to the dragon when she landed, and whom [[Queen Hesper]] commissioned that evening to find the lease and learn the rent. They have until the Lammas moon, forty days from the fair. The Crown has lost its copy. Hesper's councillors are split between paying and fighting. And somebody has been in the archive ahead of them with a razor.

## The tone

Bright, classic sword-and-sorcery, played for daylight. The campaign opened with a dragon lying along the roof of the Wool Exchange, her tail hanging down into Tanner's Row, while the town's bakers argued over who should ask her to move it. The danger is real, and so is the deadline, but in three sessions nobody at the table has drawn a sword against anything that could talk back.

## The vale

The Vale of Quillet runs south from the [[The Tally Cliffs|Tally Cliffs]], where the Brisk comes over in a single fall, down past the Sallow Hills and the market town of [[Summerhythe]] to [[Sealwright Hall]] on its island, and on to the Reed Water, where the Brisk spreads out into the lowland marshes. It is small enough to cross in three days on foot, green, well farmed and busy, and until this summer it believed it had no landlord.

## The powers

- **[[Marigold Court]].** The Crown, split between a young queen who wants to pay and a Chancellor who wants to fight, and running on borrowed money.
- **[[Worshipful Company of Sealwrights]].** The contract-mages, who kept the Crown's copy of the lease safe for 139 years and then lost the page that mattered.
- **[[Marrable and Daughters]].** The counting house that holds the Crown's notes and is very polite about it.

## The table

Fifth-edition rules (the 2024 revision), four players, fortnightly Saturdays. The party is level 3. Sessions are written up as recaps on this site, and each recap ends with what the party learned, which also appears on the [[Timeline]].

## GM Notes

The antagonist is [[Ottoline Pardew]], the silent partner behind the Marrable house. She has the cut leaf, knows that the rent is a song and that whoever tenders it holds the lease, and has hired Queen Hesper's composer to write it for her. The campaign's spine is a race: the Crown and the Wardens must pay the rent before she does, or prove the leaf was stolen, or persuade the dragon that a bought song is not "given freely." Orpiment will judge that last question herself, and she has not decided.

**Lammas must not fold in one beat.** Build at least two escalations: a rival tender on the signing stone the same night, the bought song against the Wardens' own, with Orpiment judging "given freely"; trouble on the two-day road to the cliffs, from Ottoline's agents or from Pye and Ivo moving against the dragon; and "made by the vale" as a gathering challenge, with verses to collect from the hills, the town and the marsh.

**Pressure calendar.** Day 8 to 9: the hearing on Beatrix's spurs, and the first line under the seal becomes readable. About day 15: the whole of *Of Default, and of Tender* is readable. About day 20: Pye proclaims a levy to hire dragon-slayers. About day 30: Emlyn finishes the bought song. Day 38: the last day to leave Summerhythe on foot for the signing stone. The Wardens have spent 7 days in 3 sessions; plan six to eight more sessions and skip the quiet days.

**Advancement.** Milestones: level 4 after the Jory and seal arc, around session 5; level 5 before Lammas if a High fight is planned. Session 4 should cost something: the counterpart on Iveth's worktable is the Wardens' single point of failure, and the Marrable clerk has been asking who reads old hands.
